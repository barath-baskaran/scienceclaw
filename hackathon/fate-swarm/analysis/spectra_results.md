# Spectra results, run 3: was the problem the spectral representation or the spectra? (P-002 / T-018), and what a spectrum alone can predict (P-005 / T-019)

**What this file is.** The results of the spectral work done by the W-SPECTRA agent in this run. It answers
two questions:
- Did rebuilding how MS/MS spectra are turned into model columns (the "representation") make spectra more
  useful than the previous run's representation did?
- How well can fate properties be predicted from one measured spectrum with no structure?

**Short answer.** Rebuilding the representation did not help for high-resolution ESI spectra; it helped a
little for EI spectra. A spectrum alone has some skill for water solubility, vapour pressure and logKow,
but much less than a known structure, and adding a spectrum to a known structure does not help.

**Names and paths.** Labels starting with P- or T- are the agents' internal question and task numbers. Role names:
W-SPECTRA (spectral models), W-MODEL (structure and physics models), W-SPACE (domain and leak-free
definitions), W-PHYSICS and W-TOX (reviewers), CRITIC (the agent that challenged results), the READER agents (the
literature readers) and PRINCIPAL (the coordinating role that set the rules before the runs). Files
marked `[not redistributed; VM-internal]` were working files on the event machine and are not in this
repository. Figure labels such as "Fig. S-E" refer to the agents' spectral figure set.

All numbers below were printed from the files named next to them. Figures and tables are regenerated by `report_v31.py  [not redistributed; VM-internal]` from the same files.

**Terms used throughout**
- **MS/MS modes.** HR ESI+ and HR ESI− are high-resolution electrospray tandem mass spectra in positive
  and negative ion mode. EI is electron-ionisation mass spectra (the GC-MS kind), recorded at nominal
  (whole-number) mass.
- **Error scale:** test RMSE as % of the naive guess. The naive guess is the train mean **of the same compound subset**, so 100% means no skill and lower is better.
- **Split:** the labels_v2 cluster split. Compounds were grouped into clusters of similar structures, and
  whole clusters were assigned to train, calib (calibration) or test; the split was never redrawn. Every
  spectrum of a compound sits in one split, keyed by InChIKey skeleton (the standardised structure
  identifier). No AMOS spectrum (AMOS is the spectral library used) is linked to more than one skeleton
  (checked: 0 of 268,542).
- **Skill bar** (set by PRINCIPAL before the runs): ≤85% of naive AND the upper bound of the 95% cluster-bootstrap CI < 100%. A cluster-bootstrap CI is a confidence interval made by resampling whole clusters of similar compounds, so that near-duplicates do not make the interval look narrower than it is.
- **Intervals:** split-conformal on calib, nominal 90%. Split-conformal means the interval width is set
  from the model's errors on the calibration set, so that new compounds should fall inside at the stated
  rate ("nominal coverage"). Empirical coverage, the fraction of test compounds that actually fell inside, is
  always reported next to it.
- **Representation:** `REPRESENTATION_v31.md  [not redistributed; VM-internal]`, reviewed before any fit: W-PHYSICS GO, W-TOX GO (see `chemistry_reviews.md`, R1).

## 2. P-002: old (run 2) vs new representation, same compounds, same model. A clear negative result.
Sources: `v31/eval_{pos,neg,ei}.json`; `v31/report/contrasts_v31.csv  [not redistributed; VM-internal]`; `v31/robust_control_v31.json  [not redistributed; VM-internal]`; `v31/report/old_vs_new_tally_v31.csv  [not redistributed; VM-internal]`; Figs. S-A, S-F.

A "paired CI" compares two models on exactly the same test compounds in each resample; a gain counts only
if its CI excludes zero. Δ is the improvement in points of % of naive.

- **Same-size control:** NEW-DIM (the new representation cut to the old one's size) has 745 columns; OLD has 746.
  - With the main LightGBM (gradient-boosted decision trees), NEW-DIM beats OLD (paired CI excluding 0) in 0 of 10 ESI+ cells and 1 of 8 ESI− cells (WS +6.5).
  - The full-width NEW is significant only for WS (+3.7 ESI+, +5.6 ESI−).
- **Same comparison with three other learners** on the same compounds:
  - LightGBM with different hyperparameters: NEW better in 0/10 ESI+ and 1/8 ESI− cells.
  - Winsorised ridge regression (a linear model with extreme values clipped): 2/10 and 0/8.
  - Cosine k-NN library search (predict from the most similar library spectra): 0/10 and 0/8. OLD is better in 1 ESI− cell (logKow, k-NN only).
  - Median Δ is +0.4 to +2.7 points (% of naive) across learners.
- **EI (nominal mass, quality-selected, with neutral losses and peak-pair differences)** beats run 2's EI block in 3/10 cells with both LightGBMs (BP, MP, VP: +4.5 to +6.9) and 2/10 with ridge, but 0/10 with k-NN.
- **Finding:** rebuilding the representation did NOT make spectra more useful for HR-ESI. The old representation was not the problem. The HR-ESI spectral signal is limited by what the spectra carry and by how few compounds have both a label and a spectrum, not by how the spectra were turned into features.
- **EI is the exception.** The rebuilt EI block beats run 2's on BP (n_test 216), MP (293) and VP (190) by +4.5 to +6.9 points. Caveat: EI libraries cover a biased subset of the labelled chemicals, those amenable to GC (volatile and thermally stable), and the metadata-only control (see section on CRITIC's challenges) already reaches 76% on EI VP.
- **Verdict on P-002's claim** ("Run 2's representation, not the spectra, was what failed"): **false for HR-ESI, weakly true for EI.** Run 2's file already carried the measured precursor m/z, 1-Da neutral losses and mass-defect summaries. Once those are in place, keeping the accurate fragment mass and separating high- and low-resolution spectra adds almost nothing.

### What each design change adds (one change at a time, compared with FULL; Fig. S-C)
FULL is the complete new representation. Positive Δ means the change helps; * means the CI excludes 0.

| Change | ESI+ | ESI− |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral losses + pair differences | BP +7.3*, logKow +6.9*, VP +4.4*, MP +4.2*, WS +2.5* | WS +3.6* |
| Fragment accurate mass (vs nominal) | MP +6.7* | MP +5.7*, WS +5.2* |
| Quality selection | does **not** help: MP −2.3* (random spectra slightly better), pKa-base −12.2* | pKa-acid −15.8* |

- **W-TOX caveat on accurate mass:** part of the accurate-mass gain comes from telling apart different neutral losses that share a whole-number mass (CO/N2/C2H4 at 28; ketene/propene at 42), not only from resolution as such.
- **The quality score does not sort compounds by error** (`v31/report/error_by_quality_v31.csv  [not redistributed; VM-internal]`). Prior work reports that spectral quality changes property error (doi:10.1021/acs.est.4c02833 p.1). We do not reproduce that for ESI with our structure-free quality score.

## 3. CRITIC's challenges, answered
**(P-002.1) "The gain is just the precursor mass."**
- With the shared fixed LightGBM, models that use mass as their only feature overfit and score >100%. So the mass-only model was refit with a regularised LightGBM and with a cubic ridge on log mass (`v31/mass_sensitivity_v31.json  [not redistributed; VM-internal]`).
- Regularised mass + adduct type alone, ESI+: WS 77, VP 79, logKow 89, Koc 88. MP, Henry, BP, pKa ≈ 100.
- What the fragments add on top of mass, paired CI excluding 0:
  - ESI+: logKow +15.3, MP +15.8, WS +7.3, Henry +7.4.
  - ESI−: logKow +10.6, MP +7.4, WS +9.5.
  - Not significant: VP and Koc.
- So the fragments do carry information beyond mass, but only for some endpoints.
- **Subset with theoretical (calculated, not measured) precursor m/z:** the mass-only model is not better there (`by_theoretical_precursor` in the eval json).
- **Jitter test:** adding 3 or 10 ppm Gaussian noise to the test m/z changes any model by ≤1.6 points.

**(P-002.2) "It is pooling and popularity."**
- No feature set contains counts or presence flags.
- **Metadata-only control** (a model given only spectra counts per class, instrument fractions, number of collision energies, number of sources and mean quality, with no peaks):
  - It is NOT empty. It has skill on EI VP (76%), and scores EI MP 83, EI Henry 84 and ESI+ VP 89. So which libraries hold a compound, and how it was measured, carry real information about volatility: whether a compound gets GC-EI or LC-ESI spectra is itself correlated with its chemistry (READER-1's subset-bias point).
  - The peaks beat metadata with CIs excluding 0 in 9/18 ESI cells and 7/10 EI cells (ESI+ Koc +38, logKow +28, WS +27; EI BP +25, WS +26) (contrasts META_ONLY→FULL / →EI).
  - No feature set used for prediction contains these columns.
- **One spectrum vs pooled top-5:** one spectrum is within ~5 points on all endpoints with enough data, and no CI excludes 0 apart from ESI− pKa-acid (n=34). So the pooled result is not a popularity artefact, and the dashboard ships the one-spectrum model.

**(P-005.3) "The domain flag in spectrum mode is circular."**
- The spectrum-mode familiarity flag is the highest cosine similarity of the query spectrum to any training spectrum (plus the precursor mass difference in ppm).
- **HR-ESI: uninformative.** Spearman ρ (rank correlation) with |error| is −0.11 to +0.22, with inconsistent sign. Coverage per cosine tercile is 0.75–1.0.
- **EI: weak but in the right direction** (ρ −0.03 to −0.35).
- The dashboard therefore states that the ESI spectrum-only familiarity flag is uninformative.
- The structure-based similarity nn_sim (similarity to the nearest training compound) is also weak on these subsets (ρ −0.32 to +0.18).

**(P-005.2) Subset bias.**
- Every model is scored on one compound list per endpoint×block (sha256 in the eval json).
- Structure is reported two ways: trained on the spectral subset only, and trained on all compounds (STRUCT vs STRUCT_alltrain). The all-compound model is better in 8 of 28 cells with CIs excluding 0 (by 3.8–18.1 points: BP and MP in both ESI modes, and EI BCF/Henry/MP/WS), and never significantly worse. The spectrum-vs-structure gap quoted in §1 uses the all-compound structure model, which is the realistic comparator.
- Naive is the subset's own train mean.

## 4. Spectra + structure
- Adding the spectrum to a known structure does not help: STRUCT→STRUCT+FULL Δ is −4.6 to +9.1 points, and within ±3 points on every endpoint with n_test ≥ 60.
  - Significant cells: ESI+ Henry −2.9 (worse), ESI− WS +3.0, ESI+ pKa-acid +9.1 (n=28).
  - EI: MP −1.5 (worse); the others are not significant.
- W-MODEL's independent pipeline (structure + physics + spectra) agrees: SPX−SP (the gain from adding spectra to structure + physics) has a CI below 0 in 1 of 28 cells.

## 5. Limitations recorded
- **Theoretical precursors:** Czech Acad. Sci. (164,493 of 268,542 AMOS MS records, 61%) reports a calculated rather than measured precursor m/z. The jitter tests show this does not matter.
- **Labels attached to the skeleton merge salts with their parents.** Example: the pyridine skeleton has MP 141 °C, which is a salt's value.
- **Endpoints with few compounds** (kOH, logKoa, pKa in ESI−, Koc in ESI−) are skipped or underpowered. n is stated in every table.
- **No low-resolution ESI model exists.** The predictor refuses nominal-mass ESI.
- An [M]+ ion cannot tell a permanent cation from a radical cation: 71 ESI+ library compounds with dominant [M]+ are mostly neutral aromatics. So for gas-phase and neutral-species endpoints the predictor warns rather than refuses (BB_EXPERT_NOTE_Q003, a written answer from a domain expert).

## 6. Two-stage model (spectrum → predicted substructure bits → property; the MS2Tox idea, doi:10.1021/acs.est.2c02536 p.3): ATTEMPTED, INCOMPLETE
Scripts: `twostage_v31.py  [not redistributed; VM-internal]` → `_b` → `_fix` (3 attempts). Results: `v31/twostage_pos.json  [not redistributed; VM-internal]` (attempt 1), `v31/twostage_fix_pos.json  [not redistributed; VM-internal]` (attempt 3).
- Design: stage 1 predicts which substructures are present from the spectrum and was trained only on train-split spectra. Stage 2 predicts the property from those substructures, using the same LightGBM class and parameters as the direct model (PRINCIPAL's conditions).
- **Stage 1 never learned.** Its test macro-AUC (the area under the ROC curve averaged over 381 substructure bits; a value of one half means chance) was 0.528, 0.528 and 0.525 across three attempts, trained on 21,225 spectra of 3,137 train compounds. The fixes tried were (a) removing early stopping on subset accuracy and (b) clipping after standardisation. With stage 1 at chance, this is **not a test of the two-stage idea**. It is recorded as an unresolved implementation failure of the multilabel sklearn MLP (a small neural network) on these features. The obvious next step, not done, is one LightGBM per bit, or SIRIUS/CSI:FingerID if it could be installed.
- As measured (ESI+, same test compounds and spectra as the direct one-spectrum model):
  - Stage 2 trained on the TRUE bits of all train compounds, then applied to stage-1 probabilities (TS_true): much worse than the direct model on every endpoint (e.g. logKow 100 vs 76, VP 203 vs 75).
  - The same with probabilities thresholded at 0.5: also much worse.
  - Stage 2 trained on cross-fitted predicted probabilities (TS_cross): significantly worse on logKow (−7.3), WS (−5.6) and VP (−7.1); the other endpoints are not significant.
- By the rule stated in advance, the two-stage model does not beat the direct model. Because stage 1 failed, the right conclusion is "attempted, incomplete", not "two-stage does not work".
