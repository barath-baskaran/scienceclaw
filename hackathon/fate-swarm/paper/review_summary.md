# Review summary: predicting fate and toxicity from structure and/or MS/MS spectra

*Plata lab hackathon work on environmental fate and toxicity. Built 2026-11-01 (v8) from the same result files as the whitepaper; figure numbers refer to the whitepaper. This summary is generated from the paper's own numbers; it has not itself been independently audited, and the whitepaper marks section by section which results a second agent checked.*

## What we did

A swarm of AI agents built a predictor for 12 environmental fate endpoints and a set of toxicity tasks, and then audited it. The predictor accepts three kinds of input: a structure, a structure plus a measured MS/MS spectrum, or a spectrum alone. Each prediction comes with a conformal interval, a domain flag and the EPI Suite value for comparison. Accuracy is the RMSE as a percentage of a naive guess (always predicting the training mean; 100% = no skill). We report it first on leak-free test compounds, i.e. those with no training analogue at Tanimoto >= 0.40 on Morgan radius-2, 2048-bit fingerprints. That definition is fingerprint-dependent: 57.4% of leak-free test rows still share a Bemis-Murcko scaffold with a training compound of the same endpoint (`READER-1/split_leakage_audit.md  [not redistributed; VM-internal]`), so the stricter novel-scaffold cut is reported in the whitepaper, Section 4.1. **The model that was frozen and that produces every number below is a structure + AIMNet2-physics model with no spectral features at all** (`W-MODEL/frozen_v5/columns.json  [not redistributed; VM-internal]`: two blocks, 2,296 columns, zero spectral); the spectral results come from separate models and did not produce the headline or the EPI Suite comparison.

## Hazard endpoints, on measured labels

Three hazard tasks were rebuilt on measured labels (ChEMBL dose-response for receptor potency, CompTox for rat oral LD50); the Run-2 receptor numbers, which scored against another model's consensus calls, are withdrawn. As % of the naive training-mean guess on leak-free compounds: rat oral LD50 87% (n=1012), oestrogen-receptor potency 52% (n=255), androgen-receptor potency 83% (n=149). For the rare-positive activity calls we report PR-AUC against each familiarity bin's own base rate, never accuracy: in the least familiar bin the oestrogen-receptor model reaches 0.329 against a base rate of 0.047 (precision 0.24, recall 0.44). A control that knows only the similarity bin already reaches PR-AUC ar_active 0.606, er_active 0.515, so much of the pooled ranking skill is prevalence drift rather than chemistry.

## What the tool faces outside the test split

We scored the two product questions over the EPA inventory rather than the test split: a 40,000-structure random sample for definedness and familiarity, and 635,818 inventory structures through the shipped model (of 649,665 with physics descriptors at the time the prediction pass was cut; the relaxation campaign itself reached 782,199 structures). 10% of the predicted inventory is below Tanimoto 0.3 to anything we trained on and 7% is above 0.7 - the reverse of a random test split. Up to 4.0% of the inventory is physically undefined for the gas-phase endpoints, about four times its share of the labelled data, and a further 7-16% is measurable but not equivalent to our labels; neither is a data gap. Re-weighting the hazard models by the inventory shows the test split was flattering androgen-receptor potency, and that the oestrogen-receptor intervals under-cover for the typical inventory chemical. One open problem, stated plainly: at equal Tanimoto similarity the inventory gets far wider intervals than the test set, so a similarity bin does not fully describe how far the inventory has moved from our training data.

## What failed, measured cleanly

- **Physics is a small, unstable effect.** The set of endpoints where AIMNet2 descriptors beat structure alone with a CI excluding zero changed with every defensible scoring choice (v4: henry, vapour pressure; v5 pre-de-duplication: pka_acidic, pka_basic; v5 de-duplicated: boiling_point, melting_point, pka_acidic, pka_basic), and no feature block flattens the rise of error with unfamiliarity.
- **Rebuilding the spectral representation did not rescue spectra.** Under the same model and the same compounds the rebuilt representation is no better than the Run-2 one; the old representation was not the problem. Spectra also add nothing measurable on top of a known structure.
- **The spectral domain flag does not predict error.** Median absolute rank correlation between error and the best-matching training spectrum's cosine similarity is 0.063 across 10 endpoints.
- **The model does not know transformation chemistry.** It predicts parent-to-product changes no better than changes between arbitrary similar compounds matched on size and similarity.
- **Spectrum-only fails for acute lethality, and spectra add nothing on top of a structure** (and the frozen model that produced every fate number contains no spectral features at all). The full spectrum reaches 95.8% of naive against 81.6% from the structure on the same 345 compounds - a wide interval around the training mean - and structure + spectrum minus structure spans zero. This is a well-powered negative result on the central question of the work, and it is in the main text of the paper, not a footnote.
- **Our label-fidelity bet (P-004) is falsified for the androgen receptor and not established for ER-alpha.** Models trained on measured receptor potency transferred worse to an external literature benchmark than models trained on another model's consensus labels - but the published pair compared a continuous-potency arm against a binary-label arm on a binary benchmark, which handicaps the measured arm. On the matched arm (measured ChEMBL *call*) the differences are ER-alpha [-0.117, +0.009], spanning zero, and AR [-0.193, -0.084], not spanning zero. Consensus labels are also smoother than pooled measurements, so the winning arm may have the more *learnable* labels rather than the more *correct* ones; we cannot separate those tonight.
- **Our own definedness bookkeeping was wrong for one compound class, and an expert cross-check caught it.** A standardisation step neutralised sulfonate and sulfate salts before the rule that decides whether a property exists was applied, so 6 gas-phase and neutral-species cells covering 678 skeletons were called defined when they are physically undefined; 251 labelled rows are affected. The table was rebuilt, the shipped predictor now refuses those cells, and a related SMARTS defect that gave sodium dodecyl sulfate a basic pKa of 6.6 was found and fixed in the dashboard (`CRITIC/undefined_vs_gap_cells_q003.csv`, `dashboard/tool/wtool_postproc.py`).
- **Acidic pKa has no skill on novel chemistry** and EPI Suite's AOPWIN beats us on the gas-phase OH rate constant (n=30 leak-free rows, all of them PhysProp-sourced), and we do not know why. Both are reported rather than hidden.
- **Tooling that failed:** a vendor AIMNet2 wrapper silently dropped the Coulomb term without Ewald (found and documented by OPS-2); the mechanism-matched open-shell jobs could not run because of a GPU driver/warp incompatibility; an enviPath-based transformation arm could not be built without network access; and logKoa has three leak-free test compounds, too few to score at all.


## Data priorities

These come from a leave-class-out experiment with a size-matched control, not from counting empty cells: a cell can be empty because the property is physically undefined for that class, and those cells are excluded. The largest measured novelty penalties, in points of naive error, are sulfonic/sulfate acid or salt for water_solubility (+95 pp, CI [31.0, 144.8], n=30); aromatic hydrocarbon for koc (+44 pp, CI [32.6, 57.3], n=80); sulfonic/sulfate acid or salt for logkow (+43 pp, CI [33.9, 51.6], n=25); nitroaromatic/nitro for henry (+34 pp, CI [4.2, 94.5], n=19); aromatic hydrocarbon for bcf (+28 pp, CI [13.6, 44.0], n=138); halogenated aliphatic for bcf (+28 pp, CI [5.8, 55.8], n=44). A learning-curve estimate on the same data shows that extra random measurements help familiar chemistry most, so untargeted data collection will not close these gaps; measurements inside the named classes will.


## Decisions for the team

1. **Ship the fate endpoints, gate the hazard ones.** Four fate endpoints - melting point (n=820), water solubility (403), boiling point (367) and vapour pressure (302) - beat EPI Suite on compounds with no training analogue, and they are the four that survive a Bonferroni correction over the nine leak-free comparisons that were made (`data/fate_vs_epi_suite.csv  [shipped aggregate; working file not redistributed; VM-internal]`). logKow and Koc are better unadjusted but not after correction, and we do not claim them; Henry's law and BCF are not distinguishable; EPI Suite is decisively better on the gas-phase OH rate constant (27% vs our 58% of naive, n=30) and we do not explain that away.
